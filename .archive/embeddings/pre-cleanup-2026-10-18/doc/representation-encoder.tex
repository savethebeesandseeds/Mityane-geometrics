\begin{abstract}
MTF-JEPA-MAE-VICReg is a C++20/LibTorch encoder that learns numerical
representations of sensor histories without manually labeled targets. It combines
temporal statistics and frequency information, handles missing measurements, and
produces vectors for each history and sensor channel. The core encoder and
CPU/CUDA training pipeline are implemented and working. We seek support to refine
and validate this foundation as an observation component for robotic world models.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Robotics Opportunity}
A robotic world model predicts the effects of actions. Its observation encoder
turns sensor histories into embeddings: learned numerical vectors used by a
predictor. Inputs could include joint motion, inertial measurements, motor current,
and force readings. This could complement a separate vision encoder.

\section{Representation and Learning}
The input $X$ is a rank-four real-valued array with shape $[B,C,H,F]$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l@{\quad}p{0.86\columnwidth}@{}}
$B$ & histories processed together (batch size) \\
$C$ & sensor streams or groups (channels) \\
$H$ & time steps in each history \\
$F$ & measurements per channel at each time step
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
A Boolean mask of the same shape marks each measurement as observed or missing.

MTF (multiscale time-frequency) analysis summarizes windows of several lengths
using means, standard deviations, and oscillation strengths. These summaries
become tokens: learned vectors tagged by channel, window length, time/frequency
type, and position. The shared encoder mixes information across valid tokens.
Averaging its outputs yields a vector per history and per channel; flags identify
outputs with no observations.

Learning combines four objectives. JEPA (joint-embedding predictive architecture)
predicts hidden token embeddings from visible context~\cite{assran2023}. Targets
come from a teacher: a slowly averaged copy of the token builder and encoder.
MAE (masked autoencoder) reconstructs window summaries~\cite{he2022}.
Time--frequency alignment encourages agreement between the two views. VICReg
(variance-invariance-covariance regularization) aligns lightly perturbed
views while preserving variation across examples and reducing redundant vector
dimensions~\cite{bardes2022}.

\section{Development Toward a World Model}
The proposed next step learns how embeddings change under robot actions:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{z}_{t+1}=f_{\phi}(z_t,a_t).
\end{equation}
Here $t$ is a time step, $z_t$ the current history's embedding, $a_t$ the action,
$f_{\phi}$ a predictor with learned parameters $\phi$, and $\widehat{z}_{t+1}$
the predicted next embedding. Current training reconstructs information within
an observed history; future prediction and control integration remain next steps.

A manipulation-task pilot would compare learned and hand-designed baselines on
unseen robot trajectories, measuring prediction accuracy, missing-sensor
robustness, inference time, and control utility. Training and evaluation histories
must be temporally separated to avoid overlap.

\section{Readiness and Completion}
The implementation supports CPU/CUDA training, saving and resuming training
state, and embedding export in a Debian environment. Tests cover missing data,
teacher updates, and numerical consistency. Robotics performance and real-time
suitability remain to be measured.

This implemented core demonstrates our capability to build and test
self-supervised representation systems. We seek support to refine it through
completion as a robotic world-model component, including validation on real
robot trajectories and downstream tasks.
