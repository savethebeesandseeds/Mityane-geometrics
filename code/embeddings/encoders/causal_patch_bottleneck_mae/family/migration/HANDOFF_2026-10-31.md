## Whether hour and day move the embedding

The probe is
`code/data/market/experiments/timeframe_published_bars_v1/channel_blank.cpp`.
It encodes the same 96 validation rows, then encodes them again with one
channel removed. Removal sets that channel's times to NaN, its mask to false,
and its values to zero. That is the leading pad `retain` already uses. A
removed channel keeps no finite bars. The other channels keep their own counts.
Every row stays valid. A second encode of the untouched probe matches the
first: L2 is 0 and the checkpoint state is unchanged.

A channel moves the embedding when its median relative L2 is above that repeat
and at least 0.01 of the median relative L2 from removing the five-minute
channel. Relative L2 is the embedding distance divided by the norm of the
untouched embedding.

| Condition | Mean finite bars | Median relative L2 | Median cosine | Moves |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Repeat | 32, 24, 24 | 0 | 1 | floor |
| Hour removed | 32, 0, 24 | 0.31362285936740653 | 0.95921550416862256 | yes |
| Day removed | 32, 24, 0 | 0.51066730052064235 | 0.88362767610991089 | yes |
| Hour and day removed | 32, 0, 0 | 0.67182872562029838 | 0.78819674890489233 | yes |
| Five-minute removed | 0, 24, 24 | 0.69336770377899615 | 0.75672270265603891 | scale |

The day channel moves the native embedding more than the hour channel. Removing
both moves it about as far as removing the five-minute channel. The coarser
channels are not unused padding on this checkpoint. The displacement does not
repair the trend probe. The linear head still does not beat the mean of the
average five-minute return.
