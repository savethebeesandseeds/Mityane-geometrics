## Diagnostic interpretation and limits

At the selected frozen V16/final2048 state, coefficient-0.1 temporal/NLL
gradient-norm ratios are 0.0276%–1.344% over all learned parameters and
0.0167%–0.2815% in the specified backbone subgroup. Full-vector cosines are
positive in all eight batches, while four backbone cosines are negative.
These measurements do not support broad temporal-gradient dominance or global
opposition at these points; they do show localized subgroup conflict. Three
of the negative backbone cosines are near zero; the strongest is about -0.228.

The waveform decoder accounts for 82.5%–96.6% of temporal squared-gradient
magnitude, so whole-model alignment can conceal backbone disagreement. A
single tensor has a coefficient-weighted ratio as high as 14.9%, with positive
alignment. Small global ratios do not establish that temporal supervision is
irrelevant in every parameter or over a training trajectory.

The two variance-head tensors receive Gaussian gradients and no temporal
gradients. The frozen implementation separately stops the variance-head input
gradient; Gaussian gradients still reach the backbone through the mean path.
Zero temporal variance-head gradients alone would not prove that input-stop
rule. Independently evaluated combined norms and component-derived norms agree
within about `1.315e-7` across reported groups, compatible with F32 accumulation;
norm agreement alone is not coordinatewise gradient equality.

These are raw parameter derivatives at eight fixed TRAIN batches from one
trained checkpoint. They are not AdamW-preconditioned update contributions or
a diagnosis of every point in the fresh trajectories. Per-group clip scales
are hypothetical summaries: actual training uses one global clip, not separate
clipping of the backbone or decoder groups. No clipping or optimizer step was
performed in the diagnostic.

## Reference and claim boundary

Keeping V16 seed 946103/final2048 as the selected research reference is
consistent with the failed early screen. This study establishes no new best
encoder, universal ranking, full-budget coefficient verdict or application
selection. It does not show that every possible V17 configuration is worse,
or that V16 is an ultimate encoder. Its fresh controls share changed fitting,
calibration, sampling and context choices, so differences from historical
full-budget scores cannot be attributed solely to the temporal coefficient.

The stated limits are appropriate: known January development data, only three
seed pairs, one 256-update budget, and no new reconstruction, timing, neural
head, forecasting, application-utility or fresh-population confirmation result.
The selected V16 reference retains its own historical mixed verdict and timing
limitations. January/February/March remain consumed evidence for later choices.
The negative outcome completes this bounded comparison without authorizing
longer training or changing a reference.
